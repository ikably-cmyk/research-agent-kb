**Resultado principal:** Inactivar células fúngicas (Candida spp., dermatofitos, Cryptococcus, Aspergillus) mediante la combinación de un fotosensibilizador, luz visible de longitud de onda apropiada y oxígeno, generando especies reactivas de oxígeno (ROS) que dañan múltiples componentes celulares fúngicos. Aplicable en infecciones localizadas orales, cutáneas, ungueales y periodontales/endodónticas.

- **Mycological cure rate for tinea cruris with PDT**: 8/10 (80%) patients achieved mycological cure after 1-3 treatments _(clinical: Promising but no RCTs; evidence-based review found only 7 reports covering 63 patients)_
- **Mycological cure rate for tinea pedis with PDT**: 6/10 (60%) patients achieved mycological cure _(clinical: Modest efficacy; only 3/10 had persistent healing at 8-week follow-up)_
- **Onychomycosis cure with PDT**: 11/30 (36.6%) patients cured at 18 months _(clinical: Limited long-term efficacy; additional controlled trials needed)_
- **MAL-PDT for Malassezia folliculitis (6 patients)**: 4/6 patients showed obvious improvement, 1 slight improvement, 1 no improvement after 3 sessions _(clinical: Potential alternative for patients intolerant to oral antifungals)_
- **MB-PDT in vivo for oral candidiasis (murine model)**: 500 mg/ml MB + red light totally inactivated C. albicans in oral cavity and prevented resistance emergence _(clinical: In vivo proof-of-concept; not yet validated in human RCTs)_
- **Resistance development after 20 consecutive PDT treatments (RLP068/Cl)**: No resistant mutants developed in C. albicans, S. aureus, or P. aeruginosa after 20 daily APDT passages _(clinical: Supports low resistance development potential of PDT compared to conventional antimicrobials)_

**Nivel de evidencia:** Bajo — revisión narrativa sin RCTs; evidencia clínica limitada a series de casos y ensayos abiertos (máx. 63 pacientes en revisión sistemática hasta 2010). No hay protocolos clínicos estandarizados.
